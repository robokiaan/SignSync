ISL Grammar & Gloss Rules

Indian Sign Language is its own language, not signed English. A gloss is the written shorthand for a signed sentence: one CAPITALISED English label per sign, in the order the signs are made. Every sentence in this app is written and graded by these rules.

1. Word order: time, then subject, then object, then verb (SOV)

  • Time first: TOMORROW WE SCHOOL GO, not "we go to school tomorrow".
  • Then the subject, then the object or place, and the verb last: I BOOK READ, SHE MARKET GO.
  • ISL is topic-comment: name what you are talking about first, then say something about it. CAR RED EXPENSIVE = the red car is expensive.
  • A place can open the sentence as the topic: TRAIN-STATION CROWD BIG.

2. Words that are dropped

  • No "is / am / are / was / were": HE TALL, SHE DOCTOR.
  • No articles "a / an / the": DOG BIG.
  • No "to / at / in / of" as separate signs; the relationship is shown by where the signs are placed in space: TEACHER SCHOOL GOOD.
  • No "and" between describing words; sign them one after the other: WINTER COLD WHITE.
  • No possessive 's; the owner comes first: STUDENT BOOK NEW.

3. Describing words come after the noun

  • Adjectives and colours follow what they describe: CAT BLACK, CAR RED EXPENSIVE.
  • Several describing words simply follow in a row: GRANDFATHER OLD STRONG HEALTHY.
  • Numbers and amounts also come after the noun: BOOK THREE.
  • Intensity ("very", "really") is shown by signing bigger and slower with a stronger facial expression, not by an extra word.

4. Questions and negation go at the end

  • Question words (WHAT, WHERE, WHO, WHEN, WHY, HOW) close the sentence: YOU NAME WHAT, SCHOOL WHERE, with furrowed eyebrows.
  • Yes/no questions keep normal order and are marked by raised eyebrows and a forward lean: YOU DOCTOR + raised brows = are you a doctor?
  • Negation comes last with a headshake: I HAPPY NOT, DOG BIG NOT.

5. Time and tense

  • Verbs never change form. Tense is set once by a time sign at the start: YESTERDAY I MARKET GO, TOMORROW I MARKET GO.
  • Once the time is set it holds for the whole sentence (and the following ones) until a new time sign changes it.
  • Time signs use a timeline: behind the body for past (YESTERDAY, BEFORE), at the body for present (NOW, TODAY), in front for future (TOMORROW, LATER).

6. Pronouns, plurals and space

  • Pronouns are pointing: point to yourself for I, to the person for YOU, to a set spot for HE / SHE / IT.
  • A sweep of the point makes it plural: WE, YOU (plural), THEY.
  • Plurals of nouns are made by repeating the sign or adding a number, never by an "s".
  • People and places are set up at spots in the signing space, then referred back to by pointing at that spot instead of repeating the sign.

7. The face is part of the grammar

  • Raised eyebrows = yes/no question or topic; furrowed eyebrows = wh-question; headshake = negation; nod = affirmation.
  • Mouth and cheeks show degree: puffed cheeks for BIG, pursed lips for SMALL or THIN.
  • A gloss written without these markers is incomplete; the coach grades the hands, but the face is what makes it a sentence.

8. What this app can grade

  • The INCLUDE dictionary used here has nouns, pronouns, adjectives, colours and time words, but no verb signs. Sentences are therefore written without the verb slot: TOMORROW WE SCHOOL stands for TOMORROW WE SCHOOL GO.
  • Every curated sentence's English uses only words that have a sign, so the gloss is exactly the English content words, reordered by rules 1 to 3.
  • A sentence you write yourself is first turned into its gloss by these rules (small words dropped, NOT and question words moved to the end, verbs last) and only then checked: every sign in that gloss must exist in the dictionary, or the coach shows you the gloss and names the signs it cannot grade. "I don't have food" becomes I FOOD HAVE NOT, and none of those four has a sign here.
  • The coach checks the hand poses of each word in order. Facial grammar (rule 7) is not tracked; practise it anyway.
